## Your First Avatar

The most important decision before building an offer. A structured process for selecting your first (or next) customer avatar.

**Key principle**: Who you sell to determines everything — pricing, churn, delivery cost, and lifetime value. Pick the avatar where you can deliver the most value for the least effort.

**Selection criteria** (evaluate each avatar against):
- Pain severity — how badly do they need the solution?
- Purchasing power — can they afford to pay premium?
- Accessibility — can you reach them affordably?
- Market size — is the pool deep enough to scale?
- Your authority — do you have credibility with this group?

## Section A: Attract — Promotion Types

Three promotion strategies to increase the number of people who take your offer. Each has specific trade-offs.

### Premium Promotions
- Charge *more* than competitors for the same initial offer
- Attracts highest-quality leads with best purchasing power
- Lowest volume but highest conversion and lifetime value
- Best when: you have strong proof, authority, or brand

### Free Promotions
- Remove price barrier entirely on the front-end offer
- Creates highest volume and lowest cost per lead
- Risk: attracts freebie-seekers; requires strong backend monetization
- **Friction levers** to filter quality:
  1. Increased qualifications (age, income, homeowner, etc.)
  2. Increased information requirements (long-form applications)
  3. Increased number of steps (multi-step funnels)
  4. Forced consumption (watch 40-min video before CTA appears)
  5. Advertisement length (longer ads = higher friction = better quality)
- Famous free front-ends: Facebook, YouTube, Dropbox, Uber, Netflix, Claire's

### Discount Promotions
- Lower price temporarily to create urgency
- Middle ground between premium and free
- Works best with existing audiences and time-limited windows
- Risk: can train customers to wait for discounts

**Free Money Math**: Even if 50% of free leads are unqualified, you get more *total* qualified leads than a smaller, cleaner campaign — as long as cost per qualified lead is lower.

## Section B: The Expensive Customer Problem (CFA Deep-Dive)

Extended math behind Customer-Financed Acquisition. Three levers that determine acquisition economics.

### Three CFA Levers

1. **CAC — Cost to Acquire a Customer**
   - Total advertising + sales cost ÷ customers acquired
   - Benchmark: if CAC < 3× industry average, focus elsewhere
   - Diagnostic: "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?"
     - No → advertising problem (wrong targeting)
     - Yes, but not buying → sales problem
     - Yes, and buying, but not enough → advertising problem (volume)

2. **LTGP — Lifetime Gross Profit**
   - Total revenue from a customer minus delivery costs over their lifetime
   - Increase by: raising prices, adding upsells/cross-sells, extending retention

3. **PPD — Payback Period**
   - How quickly CAC is recovered from customer payments
   - Shorter PPD = faster reinvestment = faster scaling
   - Target: recover CAC in 30 days or less

### Levels of CFA
- **Level 1**: CAC recovered within 90 days (basic)
- **Level 2**: CAC recovered within 30 days (good)
- **Level 3**: CAC recovered on day 0 — front-end offer pays for acquisition (ideal)
- **Level 4**: Front-end offer is *profitable* — you get paid to acquire customers

## Section C: Advanced Offer Stacking

### The Value Grid (Back-End Architecture)
A matrix for designing complementary products across two axes:
- **Price tiers**: Low / Medium / High / Ultra-premium
- **Delivery formats**: DIY / Done-with-you / Done-for-you / Hybrid

Each cell in the grid is a potential product. The grid ensures you capture value across all customer segments.

### Advanced Stacking Techniques

| Offer Type | Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Free Presentations** | Attraction offer via webinar/workshop that sells the core product | Free 60-min training → pitch high-ticket program |
| **Freemium** | Give core product free, monetize through premium upgrades | Free software tier → paid features/support |
| **Pick Your Price** | Let customers choose what to pay; creates goodwill and data | "Pay what you want" for digital product |
| **Free + Alternate Revenue** | Give product free, monetize through adjacent stream | Free app → ad revenue or data |
| **Lifetime Upgrades** | Continuity offer with guaranteed lifetime access to future versions | One-time fee → all future updates included |
| **Lifetime Discounts** | Lock in a permanent discount for early/loyal customers | Charter member pricing forever |
| **Discount + One-Time Fee** | Hybrid: reduced recurring + upfront setup charge | $500 setup + $99/mo (vs. normal $199/mo) |

## Section D: Expanded Employees Chapter

Extended from $100M Leads. General management and performance principles for building teams that get you leads.

### Key Principles
- Employees are the most powerful allies for scaling lead generation
- The Core Four advertising methods should be delegated to employees doing them on your behalf
- Train anyone willing; then identify and retain winners
- Don't fire sales for advertising problems (and vice versa)

### Hiring Philosophy
- For ground-level roles: hire for iron will, not brains
- Get picky only for "fancy employees" (C-suite, six-figure roles)
- Hire and train anyone willing → find winners → treat them well

### Diagnostics
- If CAC > 3× industry → identify root cause first:
  - Qualified leads not buying = sales problem
  - Unqualified leads = advertising problem
  - Too few qualified leads = volume/advertising problem

## Maker or Manager?

A framework on managing time for maximum economic output, inspired by Paul Graham's essay.

### Two Calendar Types
1. **Maker Schedule**: Large uninterrupted blocks for deep creative work. A single meeting costs 10× more because it shatters the block.
2. **Manager Schedule**: Hour-by-hour slots for meetings, decisions, and coordination. Meetings are the work.

### Key Insight
- Most business owners are makers pretending to be managers (or vice versa)
- The highest-ROI use of time depends on which mode you're in
- Protect maker time ruthlessly; batch manager time into defined windows
- "Got a min?" is the most expensive question in business

# $100M Offers — Distilled Frameworks

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* and *$100M Lost Chapters*

---

## Value Equation

The core formula for perceived value:

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

| Element | Description | How to Maximize |
|---|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | What the person wants to happen, the way they want it | State the best possible results; use proof from people like them |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | How likely they believe they'll achieve it | Results, reviews, awards, endorsements, certifications, guarantees |
| **Time Delay** | How long they think it'll take after buying | Show how fast results start, how often, how long for best results |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | Bad stuff they endure + good stuff they give up | Show what they can keep doing and still get results; what bad stuff they can skip |

**Goal**: Maximize the top two, minimize the bottom two.

## Market Selection — Four Indicators

Before crafting an offer, validate the market:

1. **Massive Pain** — They desperately need a solution (not just "nice to have")
2. **Purchasing Power** — They can actually afford to pay
3. **Easy to Target** — You can find and reach them efficiently
4. **Growing Market** — The pool of potential customers is expanding

Choose markets that score high on all four. A great offer in a bad market still fails.

## Pricing Strategy

### Virtuous Cycle of Price
Charge more → deliver more value → get better results → justify charging more → attract better clients → fewer headaches → more profit per client.

### Key Principles
- **Never compete on price.** Commoditization is a race to the bottom.
- **Value-driven purchases vs. price-driven purchases**: When value is clear and high, price becomes secondary. When value is unclear, price becomes the only differentiator.
- **Charge a premium**: Premium pricing attracts committed clients, funds better delivery, and creates a self-reinforcing quality loop.
- **Price-to-value discrepancy**: The bigger the gap between what they pay and what they perceive they get, the easier the sale.

## Grand Slam Offer Creation Process

### Step 1: Identify the Dream Outcome
What does your ideal customer want most? Be specific. Not "lose weight" but "lose 20lbs in 6 weeks without giving up the foods I love."

### Step 2: List All Problems
Write down every obstacle, concern, and perceived risk between them and the dream outcome. Think about problems:
- Before they buy
- During the process
- After they achieve the result

### Step 3: Create Solutions
Turn each problem into a solution. Each solution becomes a potential deliverable in your offer.

### Step 4: Create the Delivery Vehicles
For each solution, determine a delivery method:

| Vehicle Type | Example |
|---|---|
| 1-on-1 | Personal coaching, done-for-you |
| Small Group | Group coaching, mastermind |
| 1-to-Many | Course, membership, community |
| Self-Service | Software, templates, tools |

### Step 5: Trim and Stack
- Remove low-value, high-cost items
- Keep high-value, low-cost items
- Stack remaining items to create overwhelming perceived value
- The offer should feel like "an embarrassingly good deal"

## Offer Enhancements

### Scarcity (Quantity-Based)
Limit availability to increase urgency and exclusivity.
- **Physical scarcity**: Limited units, seats, spots
- **Cohort scarcity**: "Only accepting 5 new clients this month"
- **Ongoing scarcity**: "When a spot opens, the waiting list fills it"

### Urgency (Time-Based)
Create deadlines that force a decision.
- **Cohort-based**: "Doors close Friday"
- **Rolling deadlines**: "Bonus expires 48 hours after you see this"
- **Seasonal/event-based**: "Pre-launch pricing ends at launch"

### Bonuses (Value Stacking)
Add additional items that increase perceived value without increasing price.
- Each bonus should solve a related but distinct problem
- Name and price each bonus individually
- Stack them so total perceived value dwarfs the price
- Best bonuses: things that cost you little but are highly valued

### Guarantees
Remove risk from the buyer's decision.

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | Full refund, no questions asked | "Try it for 30 days — if you don't love it, full refund" |
| **Conditional** | Refund if specific conditions are met | "Do the work for 90 days — if you don't get X result, full refund" |
| **Anti-Guarantee** | All sales final (signals extreme confidence) | "This is not for everyone. All sales are final." |
| **Implied Guarantee** | Performance or outcome-based framing | "I'll work with you until you get [result]" |
| **Performance-Based** | Tie payment to results | "You only pay when you see [measurable outcome]" |

**Pro tip**: Stack guarantees. "If you don't get X in 90 days, I'll refund your money AND give you $500 for wasting your time."

### Naming
A great offer name communicates value instantly.
- **Formula**: [Result] + [Time Frame] + [Without Objection]
- Example: "6-Week Lean Body Accelerator — No Gym Required"
- The name IS the marketing

## Key Principles

1. **Make offers so good people feel stupid saying no** — This is the Grand Slam Offer test
2. **The offer is not the product** — The offer is the entire package: the product, the terms, the bonuses, the guarantee, the scarcity, the urgency, and the name
3. **Offers beat everything** — A mediocre product with an incredible offer outsells an incredible product with a mediocre offer
4. **Test and iterate** — Offers are living things; keep refining based on market response
5. **Starving crowd beats everything** — Find desperate buyers first, then craft the offer

## 1. Branding Playbook

**Definition**: A brand is a set of *associations* people make with your name, product, or company. Not logos, not feelings — associations that drive behavior.

### Branding vs. Advertising
- **Advertising**: "Here's my thing, buy it" — direct response, measurable
- **Branding**: Pairing your thing with associations that make people *want* to buy — indirect, compounds over time
- Great businesses do both; branding multiplies the effectiveness of advertising

### Why Branding Drives Profit
1. **Lower CAC**: Higher conversion rates from ads; brand recognition = trust = fewer objections
2. **Raise LTV**: Charge 2×–10× more for the same thing (Starbucks vs. gas station coffee)
3. **Lower Risk**: Customers stay loyal without considering competition (Apple ecosystem)

### How to Build a Brand
- **Content is the vehicle**: Volume + consistency + quality → brand associations form
- **Associations are earned, not declared**: You become what you repeatedly demonstrate
- **70-20-10 Rule**: 70% proven content, 20% adjacent content, 10% experimental
- **Audience segmentation**: Speak to where they are, not where you want them to be

### Common Mistakes
- Thinking branding = logo redesign
- Trying to brand before you have product-market fit
- Inconsistency across platforms
- Not measuring brand (track: branded search volume, share of voice, NPS)

## 2. Fast Cash Playbook

A quarterly promotion system to generate immediate cash from warm audiences.

### Core Concept
- Run Fast Cash Plays every 90 days to existing and past customers
- Create an unscalable, ultra-premium offer with genuine scarcity
- Price at 10×–50× normal price; cap the number available

### Offer Construction
- **Core**: Personal, high-touch delivery (cell phone access, custom work, etc.)
- **Bonuses**: Stack 2-3 bonuses that eliminate objections and add urgency
- **Scarcity**: Hard cap on availability (e.g., "only 5 spots")

### Execution Sequence
1. Prep all emails and texts ahead of time
2. Have bonuses ready before announcement
3. Send announcement → countdown messages with new bonus each day
4. Notify on milestones: first sales, 2 spots left, sold out, waitlist openings
5. For automated checkout: first-come-first-serve stampede
6. For consults: make available immediately, schedule within 7-day window

### 5 Reasons to Run Every 90 Days
1. Cleans up qualified-but-on-the-fence leads
2. Increases customer value (buyers buy more)
3. Shows something new and interesting is happening
4. Creates a deliverable cadence
5. All warm-lead revenue drops straight to bottom-line profit

## 3. Hooks That Get Clicks

### Why Hooks Matter
- The hook is the first impression — tailwind or headwind for everything after
- Better hooks → more clicks → better clicks → lower CAC → unlocks new markets and scale
- Bad hooks → wasted ad spend, worse customers, can't scale

### Hook Types (by audience awareness level)
| Awareness Level | Hook Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Lead with the problem or desire | "Most people don't know they're losing money on..." |
| **Problem Aware** | Agitate the specific pain | "Tired of X? Here's why it keeps happening" |
| **Solution Aware** | Differentiate your approach | "Forget everything you know about X. Here's what actually works" |
| **Product Aware** | Show proof/results | "How [Client] achieved [Result] in [Timeframe]" |
| **Most Aware** | Direct offer/deal | "Last chance: [Offer] ends tonight" |

### 120+ Hook Examples (categories)
- YouTube thumbnails and titles
- Email subject lines (book launch, scaling workshop)
- Twitter/X posts (motivational outliers)
- Landing page headlines
- Ad hooks across platforms

## 4. Lead Nurture Playbook

Maximizing 30-day show rates after leads express interest.

### Four Pillars of Lead Nurture

1. **Availability**: More open appointment slots → more shows
   - Open 7 days, 24 hours vs. limited windows
   - Accept walk-ins when possible

2. **Speed to Contact**: Respond within seconds, not hours
   - Industry average: 42 hours to respond (InsideSales.com)
   - Target: 42 seconds
   - Don't let leads schedule too far out

3. **Personalization**: Make communication relevant to each lead
   - Reference specific information they provided
   - Personalized follow-up → more responses → more show-ups

4. **Volume**: Don't give up after 1-2 attempts
   - 44% of salespeople stop after first attempt
   - Target: 5+ touchpoints per unresponsive lead

## 5. Marketing Machine (Proof-Based Content System)

A systematic process for generating unlimited ad creative from real customer results.

### 9 Components
1. **Testimonial Competitions**: Run quarterly contests for best transformation story
2. **Lifecycle Ads**: Clip before/during/after from real customer calls
3. **Social Media Scrape**: Harvest tagged content, hashtags, and UGC weekly
4. **Chat/Messaging Screenshots**: Scrape positive DMs and support messages
5. **Community Scrape**: Screenshot positive community posts and comments
6. **External Reviews**: Capture new 5-star reviews from Yelp, Google, etc.
7. **Bonus Unlocks**: Post customer milestone screenshots and videos
8. **Award Deliveries**: Document customer recognition moments
9. **In-Person Events**: Capture event footage and candid moments

### Weekly Checklist
- Names of people with results, scraped social media, chat screenshots, community posts, new reviews, bonus unlocks, award deliveries, customer reaction videos

### Implementation Order (easiest → hardest)
Competition → Chat scrape → Community scrape → Reviews scrape → Social media → Bonus unlocks → Awards → Events → Lifecycle ads

## 6. Raise Prices Playbook

### Why Raise Prices
- Doubling the price on the same product and customers = 6× profit increase (not 2×)
- 1% improvement in pricing is 4× more efficient than 1% improvement in acquisition (Profitwell study, 512 companies)
- Companies that test pricing more frequently grow faster

### Rules for Raising Prices
- Always raise prices on *new* customers immediately
- Give *existing* customers a loyalty discount window (3-6 months)
- Never lie about what you'll invest the money in
- Don't add new expenses just to justify the raise

### The RAISE Letter Framework
A template for communicating price increases:

| Step | Action | Key Message |
|---|---|---|
| **R** — Remind | Remind them of value already delivered | "Over the past 12 months, we've..." |
| **A** — Address | State the price change directly | "We need to increase our prices" |
| **I** — Invest | Show how the money improves their experience | "With these resources, we're going to..." |
| **S** — Soften | Offer a loyalty discount that expires | "We're keeping you on existing pricing for 6 months" |
| **E** — Explain | Give them a way to voice concerns | "PS: If this materially impacts you, let me know" |

### Three Customer Response Types
1. **Value-seers**: Understand and comply
2. **Actually affected**: Extend their discount window
3. **Was-gonna-cancel-anyway**: Pulled-forward churn; normalizes within 2 months

## 7. Churn Checklist Playbook

### The 5 Horsemen of Retention
Discovered by interviewing gym owners with <3% monthly churn:

1. **Track attendance**: 3×/week = sticky; <2×/week = churn risk → reach out immediately
2. **Reach out 2×/week**: Praise participation and progress; solve small problems
3. **Handwritten cards**: At signup, 3/6/12-month milestones, holidays, birthdays
4. **Member events**: Regular internal events on a calendar
5. **Community recognition**: Celebrate member wins publicly

### Tactical Churn Checklist
- Improve onboarding (resell value, use names, assign homework, schedule next call)
- Use unlockables (courses, calls, badges, status indicators) — place past average lifespan
- Community linking (introduce members, host events, create community podcast, elevate micro-celebrities)
- Fire or correct bad customers (incentivize good behavior, 3-strike rule)
- Add annual pricing options
- Implement cancellation calls (resell, tell them what they'll lose)
- Survey customers 2×/year: "Keep one, remove one" method
- ACA framework for customer check-ins
- Basic customer journey with four milestones (activation first)

### Why Churn Reduction Matters
- At 15% monthly churn: lose 83% of members in a year
- Reducing churn is the difference between a "leaky bucket" and a compounding asset

## 8. Scaling Frameworks (Acquisition.com Internal)

Five operational frameworks used internally at Acquisition.com's portfolio companies.

### 5-Star Service Framework
- Standardized system for grading customer service quality
- Score each rep against defined criteria
- Internal rubric for consistent service delivery

### Gametape Review Framework
- Record and review actual sales/service calls regularly
- Identify patterns, coach to specific moments
- "Film study" approach to continuous improvement

### High Performance Communication Framework
- Structured approach to team communication
- Templates for delivering feedback, giving direction, and resolving conflict
- Deploy across all departments for consistency

### Monday Hour One Framework
- Weekly planning ritual done at the start of every Monday
- Plan the entire week's priorities in one focused hour
- Calendar examples and templates provided
- Ensures proactive vs. reactive work patterns

### Pay Increase Framework
- Evidence-based process for employees requesting raises
- Lead with data (market research, contribution metrics), not emotions
- Step-by-step: research market rate → quantify contributions → prepare documentation → present professionally → follow up respectfully
- Limit requests to annual cycles unless exceptional circumstances

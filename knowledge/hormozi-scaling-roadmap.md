# Hormozi $100M Scaling Roadmap — Knowledge Reference

> Source: *$100M Scaling Roadmap* (Alex Hormozi / Acquisition.com, 2025)
> A 10-stage maturity model for scaling businesses from $0 to $100M+. Each stage defines the owner's role, core constraint, and graduation criteria.

---

## Stage Overview

| Stage | Name | Headcount | Owner Role | Core Constraint | Graduate By |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Improvise** | 0 | Researcher | Nothing is happening | Get people to try your stuff for free |
| 1 | **Monetize** | 1 | Starter | Business makes no money | Make your first sale |
| 2 | **Advertise** | 1 | Solopreneur | Inconsistent customer flow | Consistent sales via daily marketing (Rule of 100) |
| 3 | **Stabilize** | 2–4 | Trainer | Too much for one person | Get help; train your first employees |
| 4 | **Prioritize** | 5–9 | Manager | Trying to please everyone | Niche down; serve only your best customers |
| 5 | **Productize** | 10–19 | Director | Not enough revenue per customer (low LTV:CAC) | Make and sell a second premium product |
| 6 | **Optimize** | 20–49 | VP | Everything could be better | Fix inefficiencies; "better" beats "more" |
| 7 | **Categorize** | 50–99 | Executive | Every system is overwhelmed | Triage and organize into categories |
| 8 | **Specialize** | 100–249 | President | No one can know everything | Create dedicated specialist teams |
| 9 | **Capitalize** | 250–500 | Chairman | Plateau — not sure where growth comes from | Make a big bet on the future (M&A, R&D, new markets) |

## Stage Details

### Stage 0: Improvise
- **Goal**: Validate demand by giving product/service away free
- **Focus areas**: Build a free offer, tell people you know, collect feedback
- **Key insight**: Don't charge yet — you're in "tutorial mode." Learn if people actually want what you're offering
- **Ready to graduate when**: People are using your free stuff, liking it, and coming back for more

### Stage 1: Monetize
- **Goal**: Transition from free to paid
- **Focus areas**: Create V1 product good enough to charge for, make first sale, set up payment processing, LLC/bank account
- **Key insight**: Paying customers have higher expectations than free users — prepare for complaints and feedback
- **Ready to graduate when**: Consistently making sales, people willing to pay your prices, basic systems in place

### Stage 2: Advertise
- **Goal**: Create consistent, predictable customer flow
- **Focus areas**: Rule of 100 (100 minutes/day on marketing), CLOSER sales method, sales script, handle objections, "Angry Boat" method for complaints, "Epiphany Script" for testimonials
- **Key insight**: Marketing must be a daily discipline, not reactive. Double down on what works instead of diversifying early
- **Signs of trouble**: Inconsistent quality, helper turnover, feeling constantly behind, too many emergencies
- **Ready to graduate when**: Customers returning consistently, new customers finding you, steady revenue

### Stage 3: Stabilize
- **Goal**: Get help and stop being the bottleneck
- **Focus areas**: Hire 1–4 employees, set up payroll, fix the ONE biggest customer complaint, create onboarding, start email/content marketing
- **Key insight**: Your role shifts from "doer" to "trainer." Write down how you do things so others can replicate
- **Common mistakes**: Trying to fix everything at once, buying too many tools, informal payments
- **Ready to graduate when**: Team can handle basics without you, systems are documented, finances are tracked

### Stage 4: Prioritize
- **Goal**: Focus — stop saying yes to everyone
- **Focus areas**: Identify best customers and niche down, create CRM, track lead-to-sale conversion, employee handbook, basic insurance, P&L statements
- **Key insight**: Saying "no" to wrong-fit customers loses money short-term but enables scaling long-term
- **Mantra**: "Pick ideal customers → change product to serve them → stop trying to please everyone"
- **Ready to graduate when**: Focused customer base, measuring key metrics, professional operations

### Stage 5: Productize
- **Goal**: Create a second premium product to increase LTV
- **Focus areas**: Build product #2 from customer feedback, company-owned equipment, proper termination policies, real budgets, sales materials and recorded calls for training
- **Key insight**: One product = one-hit wonder. You need multiple products to reduce churn and increase revenue per customer. Price product #2 higher than product #1
- **Ready to graduate when**: Second product generating revenue, professional systems, budgeting and forecasting in place

### Stage 6: Optimize
- **Goal**: Make everything work better, not just bigger
- **Focus areas**: Improve core systems, train current people better, reduce CAC, real cybersecurity, detailed financial data including balance sheet, quarterly taxes
- **Key insight**: "Better" beats "more." Don't hire more — train existing people. Don't add products — improve existing ones. Don't get more tools — use current ones properly
- **Anti-patterns**: Hiring instead of training, adding products instead of improving, adding tools instead of using existing
- **Ready to graduate when**: Things work smoothly, less waste, people more productive, can grow without breaking things

### Stage 7: Categorize
- **Goal**: Sort through chaos and organize into proper categories
- **Focus areas**: Dedicated teams per feature, second customer acquisition channel (referrals), lead scoring, proactive customer success, cloud migration, HRIS, applicant tracking system, departmental budgets, spending limits
- **Key insight**: Everything feels overwhelming because you're managing it all the same way. Categorize: employees by role, leads by quality, data by sensitivity, customers by value, money by purpose
- **Ready to graduate when**: Everything properly sorted, systems handling each category, ready for specialization

### Stage 8: Specialize
- **Goal**: Replace generalists with specialists
- **Focus areas**: Survey customers to prune product features, build brand over direct response, specialist salespeople per product, separate customer service teams, ticketing systems for IT, standardized hiring process, performance management, vendor renegotiation
- **Key insight**: Like turning a high school (teachers teach everything) into a university (professors specialize). Specialists outperform generalists at this scale
- **Ready to graduate when**: Dedicated specialist teams in every function, hyper-specialization creates outsized returns

### Stage 9: Capitalize
- **Goal**: Make a big strategic bet on the future
- **Focus areas**: M&A or R&D for new products, multi-channel marketing with compliance, specialized sales by product and customer type, strategic workforce planning, audited financial statements, capital raising
- **Key insight**: Small bets won't move the needle at this size. You must make large, calculated investments (acquisitions, new markets, new product lines) with 12+ month payoff horizons
- **Key stat**: Only ~300,000 US companies reach this stage
- **Beyond**: Companies enter the large-corporation league (1,000+ employees) and paths become industry-specific

## Business Function Evolution by Stage

| Function | Early (0–2) | Mid (3–5) | Late (6–9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product** | Free → V1 → paid | Fix #1 complaint → second product | Prune features → specialize → M&A/R&D |
| **Marketing** | Tell people you know → Rule of 100 | Content/email → targeted spend | Second channel → brand-first → multi-channel compliance |
| **Sales** | Ask for money → CLOSER method → script | Materials + training → track metrics | Segment by product → specialize closers → founder-sell executives |
| **Service** | Be super responsive → Epiphany Script | Onboarding → playbook → renewal focus | Proactive success → specialist teams → separate by product |
| **Tech** | Computer + internet → basic CRM | Company equipment → dashboards | Cloud migration → HRIS + ATS → enterprise systems |
| **People** | Just you → freelancers | First hires → handbook → promote from within | Proper HR → recruiting firms → strategic workforce planning |
| **Money** | Track basics → QuickBooks | P&L → budget → forecasting | Balance sheet → quarterly taxes → audited statements → capital raise |

## Diagnostic: "What Stage Am I At?"

Use these signals to identify a business's current scaling stage:

1. **No revenue, no product**: Stage 0 — validate with free offers
2. **Revenue but inconsistent**: Stage 2 — need daily marketing discipline
3. **Founder doing everything**: Stage 3 — need to hire and document
4. **Saying yes to everyone**: Stage 4 — need to niche down
5. **Customers leave after one purchase**: Stage 5 — need a second product
6. **Growing headcount but not profit**: Stage 6 — optimize, don't add
7. **Everything feels chaotic**: Stage 7 — categorize and organize
8. **Generalists hitting ceiling**: Stage 8 — specialize
9. **Revenue plateaued at scale**: Stage 9 — make a big bet
